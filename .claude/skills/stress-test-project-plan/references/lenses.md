# Lenses and checklists for the project-plan stress test

## Assumption types (validate in this order)

1. Problem: do they have it, often, painfully?
2. Demand: will they pay / switch from what they do now?
3. Reachability: can we find them at a cost that works?
4. Supply: can we actually get the goods / data / partner on acceptable terms?
5. Solution: can we deliver the promise reliably?
6. Economics: does one order make money after returns, fees, support, data cost?
7. Legal: licences, consumer law, data protection, product liability.

## Mental lenses (pick 3, write one sentence each)

- Inversion: the surest way to kill this is … so guard against …
- Power law: of everything in the plan, the one thing that matters most is …
- Work backwards: write the customer's one-line review 12 months from now; what had to be true?
- Via negativa: what should be removed from the plan to make it more likely to work?
- Skin in the game: who in the plan loses if it fails, and who merely advises?
- Comparison set: what will the customer compare us to, and on what three attributes?

## The Five Killers (early-stage)

1. Building before validating.
2. No clear customer (everyone = no one).
3. Running out of cash or time before the first real signal.
4. A dependency the founder does not control (partner, platform, data) changing terms.
5. Founder misreading polite interest as demand.

Check each against the plan and say which one is most live.

## Dependency prompts for a parts-commerce plan

- Catalog data provider: pricing, quota, licence to cache/display, uptime, coverage per
  market, what happens if they go away or triple the price.
- Vendor: exclusivity, price changes, stock accuracy, shipping reliability, returns policy,
  channel conflict with their own online efforts.
- Payment provider and chargebacks.
- Courier reliability and cost per parcel, especially heavy parts (discs, batteries).
- Consumer protection: return rights, warranty obligations, who is liable for a wrong part
  causing damage.
- VIN / personal data handling under local privacy law.

## Gate falsifiability test

A gate is real only if (a) it has a number, (b) the number is set before the phase runs,
(c) there is a concrete action if it fails (stop, pivot, fix), and (d) the founder has said
out loud what they will do when it fails. Gates missing any of these are theatre.
